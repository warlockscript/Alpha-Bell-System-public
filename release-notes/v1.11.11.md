**You can see what you clicked — and a new Quick Play.**

This is the first release published here. It includes everything from 1.11.10, which was never shipped on its own.

## Fixed

- **Selected rows are visible again.** Clicking a task in *Today's Schedule*, a *Preset Schedule*, a *Media Player* track, an entry in the calendar's day list, or a group or saved schedule in *Schedule Center* now marks it with a tinted background and an outline. In the light theme the selection used to be white, so nothing showed which row you had picked — easy to Edit or Delete the wrong one. In the dark theme it was a pale blue under light-grey text that could barely be read. The text now changes colour with the highlight, in every theme.
- **The Operator Monitor stops when you press Stop.** Pressing Stop in Quick Play or the media player used to silence the zones but leave the Operator Monitor playing until the file ended.
- **Quick Play no longer says "Playing" after its zones were taken.** When an emergency or a page took over the zones Quick Play was using, the window kept showing the track as playing. It now says the track has ended.
- **The Quick Play playlist can be filled.** The old playlist buttons did nothing. Use *Browse…* (several files at once) or drag audio files onto the window; emergency tones are refused.

## Improved

- **Quick Play has a new layout.** A compact player beside the playlist, and a card per zone with an ON/OFF switch that says what the zone is doing right now — *Playing*, *Routed*, or which other part of the system is using it (a bell, paging, an emergency). Quick Play now follows your light or dark theme.

## Changed

- **Closing Quick Play stops what it was playing** and clears its playlist, so a track can never keep playing with no Stop button on screen. Only Quick Play's own audio stops — bells, the media player, paging and emergency tones carry on.

## Reminder: power-loss recovery

The bells only come back after a power cut if Windows signs in by itself. *Help → System Health → Power-Loss Recovery* tells you whether this computer can recover on its own. See the README for details.

**Not a certified fire alarm.** Alpha-C Bell System is a supplementary notification aid — see section 1 of the licence agreement.
